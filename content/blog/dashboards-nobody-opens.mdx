Every company has a dashboard someone spent two days building that has been opened four times since. The reasons are consistent and none of them are about the charts.

It has no owner. A dashboard belonging to everyone belongs to nobody, and when a number moves there is no one whose week it changes. Put a name on it.

There is no threshold. A chart that shows a line going along tells you nothing about whether today is normal. A chart with a line and a stated expectation — we expect this above four hundred — turns every glance into a yes or no rather than a judgement call.

Nothing brings anyone to it. Dashboards do not compete well against inboxes. The ones that get used are the ones that push: an alert into the channel where the team already is, a weekly digest that arrives without being asked for. The dashboard is where you go once something has already told you to look.

And most of them answer questions nobody asked. A dashboard assembled from the metrics that were easy to add is a different object from one assembled from the three numbers a team argues about. Build the second kind, and delete the first.
