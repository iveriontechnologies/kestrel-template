The advice written for companies with data teams does not transfer. Warehouse-first, dbt models, a semantic layer, an analyst who owns definitions — all reasonable, all irrelevant if there are eleven of you and nobody has the word data in their job title.

What matters instead is that the people asking questions can answer them without filing a ticket. That means three things.

Definitions live in one place and are visible. If "active user" means something different in two dashboards, every meeting about the number becomes a meeting about the definition. Name the events, write down what they mean where people can see it, and resist having two.

The tool has to be usable by whoever has the question. If answering anything requires SQL, questions get batched, batched questions get deprioritized, and the loop closes once a month instead of once an hour. The speed of the loop matters more than the sophistication of any single answer.

Export is non-negotiable, even before you need it. The day you hire an analyst they will want the raw events in a warehouse, and a tool that makes that hard turns a good hire into a migration project.

None of this requires a data team. It requires deciding, early, that answering a question should take minutes.
